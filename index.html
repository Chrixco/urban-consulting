Mandate

Cities face concurrent pressures from climate hazards, demographic change, health risks and fiscal constraint. Municipal responses are frequently organised by sector, with resilience, mobility, public health and green infrastructure handled by separate departments and separate instruments. The result is a gap between policy commitment and territorial outcome.

Proxima Urban works at this gap. The firm combines applied research in urban and regional planning with instrument design for public administration. Each engagement produces a documented baseline, a set of spatially explicit targets, and the governance arrangements required to sustain them across electoral cycles.

Service lines

Four service lines, each defined by an explicit analytical framework, a measurable target and a delivery instrument for the municipality.

01

Urban resilience planning

Multi-hazard risk assessment and resilience strategy at municipal and metropolitan scale, with emphasis on peri-urban fringes, watershed systems and nature-based solutions. Outputs are aligned with the Sendai Framework and structured for reporting under the UNDRR Making Cities Resilient 2030 initiative and ISO 37123.

  • Hazard, exposure and vulnerability mapping
  • Resilience strategy and action plan
  • Nature-based solutions portfolio and financing pathway
  • Indicator framework and monitoring protocol
02

15-minute city and proximity planning

Accessibility diagnostics measuring walking and cycling access to six functional domains: living, working, commerce, health care, education and leisure. The analysis identifies deficit areas, tests land use and mobility interventions, and translates results into zoning, public facility and street design instruments.

  • Network-based isochrone analysis by population group
  • Service provision gap assessment
  • Neighbourhood intervention scenarios
  • Integration with master plans and mobility plans
03

One Health City certification (OHC)

A certification framework developed by Proxima Urban that assesses how municipal planning, environmental management and public health systems address the interdependence of human, animal and ecosystem health. The framework is aligned with the WHO, FAO, WOAH and UNEP One Health Joint Plan of Action and with the URBACT One Health 4 Cities network.

  • Baseline assessment across governance, land use, water, biodiversity and health surveillance
  • Three certification levels with verified indicator thresholds
  • Cross-departmental governance protocol
  • Periodic re-assessment and public reporting

04

Green affordance: 300 m access

Assessment and planning for universal access to public green space of at least 0.5 hectares within 300 metres of every residence, following the WHO Regional Office for Europe benchmark and the 3-30-300 rule. The service quantifies current coverage, prioritises underserved areas and defines the land, budget and regulatory measures required to close the gap.

  • Parcel-level accessibility and quality audit
  • Tree canopy and visibility indicators
  • Site acquisition and conversion priorities
  • Green space standards for planning regulations

One Health City certification

The OHC framework evaluates five domains. Each domain contains indicators with defined data sources, thresholds and verification procedures. Certification is awarded at one of three levels and is valid for three years.

Governance

Existence and functioning of a cross-departmental One Health coordination body with planning, environment, health and veterinary competences.

Land use and habitat

Protection of ecological corridors, control of urban expansion into high-biodiversity areas, and management of the human–wildlife interface.

Water and sanitation

Watershed protection, wastewater treatment coverage, stormwater management and surveillance of water-borne risks.

Health surveillance

Integrated monitoring of zoonotic, vector-borne and environmental health risks, with data sharing between municipal and regional authorities.

Public space and food systems

Green space access, urban heat exposure, animal welfare in public space, and regulation of urban food production and markets.

Certification levels

LevelRequirement
CommittedGovernance body established; baseline completed in all five domains; action plan adopted.
AdvancingMinimum thresholds met in at least three domains; monitoring system operational.
CertifiedMinimum thresholds met in all five domains; two consecutive monitoring cycles reported publicly.

Approach

Engagements follow a four-phase structure. Each phase closes with a deliverable that the municipality can adopt formally, independent of subsequent phases.

  1. 1

    Diagnostic

    Territorial baseline using municipal data, remote sensing and field verification. Indicators are computed at parcel, neighbourhood and municipal scale.

    Deliverable: baseline report and indicator atlas

  2. 2

    Strategy

    Targets, priority areas and intervention portfolio, tested against budget, land availability and regulatory competence.

    Deliverable: strategy document for council adoption

  3. 3

    Instruments

    Translation of the strategy into zoning provisions, design standards, procurement criteria and inter-departmental procedures.

    Deliverable: draft regulatory and administrative instruments

  4. 4

    Monitoring

    Indicator protocol, data pipeline and reporting cycle, with training for municipal staff and external verification where certification applies.

    Deliverable: monitoring manual and first annual report

Multi-scalar analysis

Hazards, service networks and ecological systems operate across municipal boundaries. Diagnostics are conducted at the scale at which each system functions, and instruments are assigned to the institutional level with competence to act.

Institutional fit

Recommendations are limited to measures within the legal and fiscal capacity of the client. Where competence lies elsewhere, the deliverable specifies the inter-governmental arrangement required.

Verifiable targets

Every target is expressed as an indicator with a defined data source, computation method and reporting interval, so that progress can be audited by the municipality and by third parties.

Reference frameworks

Deliverables are structured to be compatible with the following international frameworks and standards, so that municipal reporting obligations are met through the same indicator base.

Sendai Framework for Disaster Risk Reduction 2015–2030
Risk reduction targets and priorities for action at local level.
UNDRR Making Cities Resilient 2030
Resilience roadmap stages and the Disaster Resilience Scorecard for Cities.
ISO 37120, 37122 and 37123
Indicators for city services, smart cities and resilient cities.
UN Sustainable Development Goal 11 and the New Urban Agenda
Targets 11.3, 11.5, 11.7 and 11.b on land use, disaster losses, public space and integrated policies.
WHO Regional Office for Europe, urban green space guidance
Benchmark of public green space of at least 0.5 ha within 300 m linear distance of all residences.
3-30-300 rule
Three trees visible from each dwelling, 30 percent canopy cover in each neighbourhood, green space within 300 m.
One Health Joint Plan of Action 2022–2026
WHO, FAO, WOAH and UNEP framework for integrated human, animal and environmental health.
URBACT One Health 4 Cities
European network and guidebook on One Health governance and implementation in municipalities.
15-minute city framework
Proximity-based planning model formalised by Moreno et al. (2021) and adopted by the C40 Cities network.
