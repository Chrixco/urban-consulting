00 — Mandate

The implementation gap lies between integrative commitments and the instruments that regulate land use.

Cities hold integrative commitments on climate, health and sustainable development, framed by SDG 11, the New Urban Agenda and national climate policy. Progress on these commitments remains uneven. The gap rarely stems from an absence of plans. It is produced by misalignment across governance scales and by the way commitments are translated, or neutralised, in zoning categories, regulatory rules and institutional routines.

Proxima Urban works on this translation. Each engagement identifies planning disjunctions, understood as situations in which commitments expressed in plans are weakened or contradicted by regulatory categories, institutional routines or spatial decisions. The findings are converted into instruments, indicators and governance arrangements that a council can adopt and audit.

01 — Service lines

Two service lines, tested through scenario exploration. One method from analysis to adopted instrument.

Each service treats the territory as a multiscalar socio-ecological system and applies one analytical framework with four dimensions: governance, ecological, urban and socioeconomic.

01Proximity · Exposure · Biodiversity

One Health City assessment

Lead · Dr.-Ing. Christian Xavier Corral Burau

An assessment of how urban form, green–blue infrastructure and environmental exposure shape the health of people, animals and ecosystems. The assessment applies the One Health City framework and index developed at the Institute of Landscape Architecture, Leibniz Universität Hannover, at metropolitan, district and street scale.

Scope

  • Proximity diagnostics: walking access to daily services against the 15-minute benchmark, and to public green space against the WHO Europe 300 m benchmark
  • Exposure mapping: heat, noise and air pollution by population group
  • Biodiversity, habitat connectivity and the human–wildlife interface
  • Future scenarios and priority areas for intervention

Value for the city

  • Directs investment in green space and public facilities toward the neighbourhoods with the largest health deficit
  • Reduces exposure-related health costs, including heat illness and physical inactivity
  • Establishes one indicator baseline for health, climate and biodiversity reporting

02SDG 11 · Sendai · EU Mission Adaptation

Climate resilience and nature-based solutions planning

Lead · Dr Andresa Lêdo Marques

Resilience understood as transformation, not as incremental risk management. The service addresses the drivers of exposure in land-use regimes, rules and incentives, and the governance relations between municipal, intermunicipal and metropolitan scales. It focuses on metropolitan fringes and shared watersheds, where environmental assets, infrastructure deficits and social vulnerability overlap.

Scope

  • Plan alignment audit: municipal zoning cross-walked with metropolitan macro-zoning and environmental zoning in a harmonised zoning mosaic
  • Identification of planning disjunctions through document analysis, spatial evidence and interviews with municipal and regional actors
  • Green–blue infrastructure planned as a continuous network: ecological corridors, riparian buffers and slope stabilisation across municipal borders
  • Resilience strategy, intermunicipal arrangements and compensatory instruments such as payment for ecosystem services and watershed funds

Value for the city

  • New development steered toward serviced, lower-risk locations, which avoids lock-in to flood, landslide and heat exposure
  • Nature-based solutions backed by mapped networks, performance targets and budgets, which moves them from symbolic compliance to ecological function and reduces grey infrastructure costs
  • Funding-ready documentation for EU, Horizon Europe and development bank programmes

03IPCC SSP · Adaptation pathways · Participatory foresight

Scenario exploration

Lead · Dr.-Ing. Christian Xavier Corral Burau, with Dr Andresa Lêdo Marques

Structured exploration of plausible futures for climate, land use and population. Plans and investments are tested against these futures before the council adopts them. Scenario exploration is a required step of climate resilience planning and extends the One Health City assessment to future conditions. It can also be contracted as a stand-alone service.

Explore the scenarios

Scope

  • Explorative scenarios combining climate projections from the IPCC shared socioeconomic pathways with local land use and demographic trends
  • Spatial modelling of flood, heat and health exposure under each scenario
  • Adaptation pathways: measures to commit now, measures to defer, and the thresholds that trigger each next step
  • Participatory scenario workshops with municipal departments and stakeholders

Value for the city

  • Avoids lock-in to infrastructure that fails under future conditions
  • Sequences investment: commit to measures that perform in every scenario, defer the rest until conditions require them
  • Gives the council a transparent basis for long-term decisions across electoral cycles

All services follow the same four-phase method. They can be contracted separately or as one integrated assessment.

04 — One Health City framework

The city as one system for people, animals and ecosystems.

The One Health City framework evaluates and envisions more ecological and democratic urban futures across species. It was developed at the Institute of Landscape Architecture, Leibniz Universität Hannover, and presented at the AESOP 2026 workshop. Proxima Urban applies the framework in municipal practice in agreement with the institute.

  1. 1

    Actors

    Human and non-human inhabitants of the city, their needs, vulnerabilities and interactions.

  2. 2

    Natural environment

    Green and blue infrastructure, habitat, water, soil and microclimate.

  3. 3

    Built environment

    Urban form, density, mobility networks, services and public space.

05 — Approach

Four phases. Each closes with a deliverable the municipality can adopt formally.

  1. 01

    Analysis

    Territorial baseline built by triangulating document analysis of plans and regulations, spatial evidence and interviews with municipal and regional actors. Indicators are computed at parcel, neighbourhood and municipal scale.

    → Baseline report, zoning mosaic and indicator atlas

  2. 02

    Scenarios

    Future scenarios for climate, land use and population, with intervention options compared by cost, avoided loss and health benefit.

    → Costed strategy for council adoption

  3. 03

    Adoption

    Translation of the strategy into zoning provisions, design standards, procurement criteria and intermunicipal arrangements, so that commitments acquire legal and operational force.

    → Draft regulatory and administrative instruments

  4. 04

    Monitoring

    Plan alignment audit repeated at fixed intervals, with territorial indicators such as the annual change in ecological connectivity and the share of permits issued in low-risk areas.

    → Monitoring protocol and first audit report

Multi-scalar analysis

Watersheds, ecological corridors and mobility systems are functions of common interest that operate across municipal boundaries. Diagnostics are conducted at the scale at which each system functions, and instruments are assigned to the institutional level with competence to act.

Institutional fit

Recommendations are limited to measures within the legal and fiscal capacity of the client. Where competence lies at another scale, the deliverable specifies the intermunicipal or metropolitan arrangement required.

Verifiable territorial outcomes

Every target is expressed as an indicator with a defined data source, computation method and reporting interval. Evaluation measures the coherence of plans and their territorial effects, not the presence of plans alone.
